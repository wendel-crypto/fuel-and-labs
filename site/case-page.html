Fuel & Labs

Two companion apps for a marathon build. Fuel turns every training day into carbs, protein, meals, recipes and a weekly grocery cart. Labs turns seven blood panels into one clear view of what to fix. Built with Claude, fed by Garmin and kept fresh by scheduled agents.

The problem

A training plan says how far. It never says what to eat.

Marathon fueling is arithmetic most runners skip: grams of carbohydrate per kilo of body weight, different for a rest day, a 5:30 am track session and a 32 km long run, with top-ups before the big days and a carb load before the race. Doing it by hand every day does not last.

Blood work has the opposite problem: plenty of numbers, no story. Seven PDFs from two labs over three years, each with its own layout and units, and no easy way to see which markers are drifting and what to ask the doctor.

So the two apps share one job: read the data that already exists and say what to do today.

Both apps install to the home screen and open full screen. The day strip, macros and weather fit above the fold on a phone; meals stack into photo cards with the recipe one tap away.

How it runs

Data in, decisions out, agents in between.

01

Garmin sets the targets

The private version reads the median of the last ten Garmin weigh-ins, so carbs, protein and fat move with body weight instead of a number typed once.

02

The plan drives the menu

Each of 154 days has a session type and distance. Rules turn it into grams per kilo, add a top-up before long runs of 24 km or more and a 10 g/kg carb load for the last three days.

03

A Friday grocery agent

Every Friday at 7:40 am a scheduled Claude task reads next week’s sessions, fills an Instacart cart and sends the link. Checkout stays with the human; nothing is bought automatically.

04

A 4 am weather agent

A daily task saves the Miami forecast before the run. The morning low becomes a fluid tip: humid starts get 500 ml more and an extra electrolyte stick.

05

Seven PDFs, one table

Claude read seven lab reports from two labs and normalized them into one table: marker, date, value, unit, lab range and flag. Everything in Labs is drawn from that file.

06

Athlete targets, not just lab ranges

A value can be inside the lab range and still worth watching for an endurance athlete. Labs adds a second, stricter target and a separate amber state for it.

Design decisions

Six choices that make the numbers readable at a glance.

01

The date is the headline

A 128-pixel day number, the weekday and the session sit first, so the app answers “what is today” before anything else.

02

The week lives inside the hero

Seven day tiles with an icon and kilometers sit in the hero, so changing day never scrolls the page.

03

Weeks, not 154 bars

The first draft charted every day and was unreadable. The build is now 22 rows grouped by phase: weekly kilometers as a bar, average carbs as a number, the long run on the right.

04

Food you can see

Every meal has a photo, a time, macro chips and a folded recipe with prep minutes. Dishes are AI-generated; juices are Unsplash photos, credited on the card.

05

Three states, one legend

Labs uses red for flagged by the lab, amber for outside the athlete target and green for in range, in the ring, the heatmap, the cards and the charts alike.

06

Every number shows its source

In the private dashboards each figure links back to the row or calculation it came from, so a coach or a doctor can check it.

Design system

Ocean navy, one working blue, sans-serif throughout.

The palette moved from forest green to ocean blue to match Miami mornings: a navy-to-teal gradient for heroes, blue for actions, teal for hydration, amber for carbs and a fixed set of session colors (teal easy, violet track, blue long run, coral race).

My role

Product, art direction and data. Built with Claude.

I set the brief, the fueling rules and the visual direction, and reviewed every iteration, from the first green dashboard to the blue redesign. Claude read the lab reports, wrote the apps, generated the dish images and runs the Friday and morning agents.

The public build uses sample data for a demo athlete, Leo: 70 kg, with lab values shifted from the originals. It is a design and product case, not medical advice.
